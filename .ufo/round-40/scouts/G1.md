The registry `$comment` states the bump-in-step rule as: "edit this file, both
in-file tables and the guard docstring in the same change; a half-bump now
fails loudly on the side that was missed." The first three carriers are
machine-checked against each other (directions A–D above). The **docstring is
not checked by anything live**:


i.e. a guard whose docstring claims a floor that contradicts the tables and
the registry still reads exit 0 all-PASS. Red-before basis: the live guard
never parses its own docstring (grep for the floor list in
`verifiers/check_assertion_floors.py` matches only the docstring itself), and
no other instrument reads it (`grep -rln "floors-registry|check_assertion_floors"
verifiers/*.py` → only the guard). Prior-round check: the r37 `floors.in_step`
proof (V7.md:55) parsed the docstring, but it was a one-off r37 proof script,
not a live gate; no scout report in r37–r39 files the silence of a
docstring-only drift.

**Why it matters (the seat's own remit):** the docstring is the only
human-readable record of the floors; the r35–r39 record shows the docstring
lagging repeatedly in-flight (e.g. r39 V4.md:142 caught it at 129/68 while the
registry still read 119/59 — that time the registry side was the loud one).
The docstring is also the carrier most likely to be edited by hand in a
"quick" fix. Severity is bounded: it is prose, no instrument reads it, and my
watcher now covers it — but the registry comment's claim is overstated for
this carrier.

**Disposition for the parent:** either (a) extend the guard to parse its own
docstring floor list and cross-check it (small; the same regex my watcher
uses), or (b) reword the registry `$comment` to say the docstring is a
carrier maintained by convention, not a machine-checked one. Either is a
one-claim move; I did not edit the guard myself (not my seat, and the
single-writer hazard applies to the registry comment).
