## Findings

**F1 — the round numbers.** loop-round N = round 20+N, three independent ways (cmds 2, 3, and the report headers), so loop-rounds 7-8 = **rounds 27-28**. The loop is 14 rounds long: round 21 opens it and `loop-round 14` is the git-correctness audit (`r25 claims.json:31`; `.ufo/round-26/scouts/X15.md:4,36`). Rounds 27-28 therefore sit mid-loop, after the loop-round-5 verification sweep and four rounds before the audit; no recorded rule puts a sweep at 7-8.

**F2 — the axis menu is fixed and the lowest axis is a tie.** At HEAD, depth 9 (owned) and consistency 10 (owned) sit above coverage/operability/calibration 8.5, with **freshness 8.0 = adjacency 8.0 the tied-lowest non-retired pair** (cmd 4). Every round since 21 worked that pair (r21 F/A, r22 B/A2, r23 P/A, r24 E/H, r25 V1/V2, r26 R/I). Rounds 27-28 inherit the constraint unless R/I move a score — and neither can: R repairs a guard inside a corpus doc, I repairs an exit path; both are repair-holds on their own axis.

**F3 — round 27 is nudge-eligible but dormant, so no waiver pressure.** By C2's arithmetic the window completes at r26 close **iff** no capability row enters (cmd 6); neither R nor I carries the `type` key (cmd 7), and r24's precedent is explicit — a repair of an existing instrument landed as an operability claim, not a capability row. Even at 3/3 the nudge stays dormant: `AXES.md:68-70` needs *every owned axis* at the line and depth is 9 < 10.0. So rounds 27-28 keep the normal shape — 1-3 claims, at least one on the lowest axis — and a capability claim is optional, not forced.
