## Authoring decisions learned

- Exact identity preserves the reference aggregation boundary. Silently lowercasing, trimming or Unicode-normalizing would merge distinct inputs and conceal what was actually authored. The tool uses exact equality; paraphrases and semantic split leakage stay human work.
- Conflicting labels require the author to resolve intent. Choosing the first/last row would make execution deterministic but might silently pick the wrong target. The aid reports both indexes and does not rewrite either input.
- Equal-label duplicates still matter: they change aggregation/weight even without a label conflict. Distinguish them diagnostically, but refuse silent deduplication in both cases.
- A threshold boundary is advisory, not invalid input. Exact rational arithmetic reports reachability and trigger count; it never invents a comparator or claims the guide settles equality.
- A mechanical aid can earn a bounded mechanical claim. Independent complete-output cases and cheap shortcut controls are appropriate evidence; these do not become model routing or semantic quality evidence.
